How Keepers Work

Checked against 101 picks

One number follows a player for as long as anyone holds him. It gets two rounds more expensive every season, and when it would cost better than a first-round pick, he goes back in the pool.

A waiver pickup's full life
RD 11→ RD 9→ RD 7→ RD 5→ RD 3→ RD 1 · last year→ Pool

Where it starts

Two rounds better than where he was drafted. A round 9 pick keeps at round 7. Waiver pickups start at round 11.

It climbs

Hold him another season and the cost drops two more rounds. No cap on years — the ladder runs out on its own.

When he's gone

Once the next cost lands past a first-rounder he isn't keepable. That's why rounds 1 and 2 are locked the moment they're drafted.

The number is his

Trade him or claim him off waivers and the cost comes along. Nobody resets a keeper clock by cycling a player.

Two per team

Each keeper takes that round's pick, so keeping at round 3 costs you your third-rounder.

Locked before playoffs

The frozen roster decides eligibility. Drops during the playoffs don't cost you a keeper.

The Roster Lock

Why the snapshot matters

Rosters lock before the playoffs, and that frozen roster — not whatever the team looks like in April — is what keeper eligibility is judged from. Yahoo doesn't preserve it, which is why it currently lives as screenshots pasted into a spreadsheet.

What gets frozen

All ten rosters exactly as they stand at the lock, bench and IL included.

What it decides

Who each manager may keep. Moves after the lock add and remove nothing.

Why it exists

Playoff teams churn rosters chasing a title. Without the lock, chasing a ring would cost you keepers.

What the site will do

One button, one timestamp, all ten rosters kept forever and still readable years later.
